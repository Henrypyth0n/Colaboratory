The Fundamentals of Debit (DR) & Credit (CR)

    
Every transaction affects at least two accounts and must always keep the basic accounting equation in balance:

    
Assets = Liabilities + Equity


    


    
1. Normal Balances (The Increase Side)

    
A **Normal Balance** is the side (DR or CR) that **increases** the account's balance.


    

        

            Type
            Debit (DR) Action
            Credit (CR) Action
            Transaction Examples
        

        

            A S S E T S (Cash, A/R)
            Increases (DR+)
            Decreases (CR-)
            
                DR Cash: Received cash from a customer. 

                CR Cash: Paid a bill or bought equipment.
            		
        

        

            L I A B I L I T I E S (A/P, Notes Payable)
            Decreases (DR-)
            Increases (CR+)
            
                DR A/P: Paid off a supplier's invoice. 

                CR A/P: Bought inventory/supplies on account.
            		
        

        

            R E V E N U E S (Sales, Service)
            Decreases (DR-)
            Increases (CR+)
            
                DR A/R or Cash: Received payment for service.

                CR Revenue: Completed a service for a customer.
            		
        

        

            E X P E N S E S (Rent, Salaries)
            Increases (DR+)
            Decreases (CR-)
            
                DR Expense: Incurred or paid a cost (rent, wages).

                CR Cash: Paid the expense immediately.
            		
        

        

            E Q U I T Y (Stock, R/E)
            Decreases (DR-)
            Increases (CR+)
            
                DR Dividends: Paying out owner distributions. 

                CR Common Stock: Owner invests cash into the business.

2. Key Concepts: The "On Account" Rule

    
When you see **"on account,"** it means the transaction involves **credit** (a promise to pay later).

    
    
        
  • If you **buy** something **on account**: You haven't paid cash. **Credit** an increase to **Accounts Payable** (Liability $\uparrow$).
    • 
        
  • If you **sell** something **on account**: You haven't received cash. **Debit** an increase to **Accounts Receivable** (Asset $\uparrow$).
    • 
    
  • JOURNAL ENTRY RULE: To record an Expense, you always Debit the Expense account and Credit the Accumulated Depreciation account. Never reverse these for recording depreciation.
  • 
    4/10 (Four-ten): The buyer can take a 4% discount if they pay the invoice within 10 days of the invoice date.
    n/30 (Net 30): The full amount (net) of the invoice is due within 30 days of the invoice date, with no discount offered. 
    • 
    1. The Gross Method (Commonly Used)
    Description: The Gross Method records Accounts Receivable and Sales Revenue at the full invoice amount initially, completely ignoring the available discount.

    Adjustment: If the customer takes the discount (pays early), the discount amount is recorded as a Sales Discount (a contra-revenue account) when the cash is received.

    2. The Net Method
    Description: The Net Method records Accounts Receivable and Sales Revenue at the net amount (the full amount minus the maximum available discount) immediately.

    Adjustment: If the customer does not take the discount (pays late), the amount of the forfeited discount is recorded as a separate Sales Discounts Forfeited (an other revenue account).

    
Always remember to keep Total Debits equal to Total Credits!
